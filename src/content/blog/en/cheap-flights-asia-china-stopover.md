Most people booking flights from the US or Europe to Japan, South Korea, or Southeast Asia ignore one of the best travel hacks available: **routing through China**.

Chinese airlines like China Eastern, China Southern, and Air China consistently offer some of the cheapest transpacific fares. The catch? You have a layover in China. But since 2024, that "catch" became a feature — because China now lets citizens of 55 countries stay **up to 10 days visa-free** during transit.

That means cheaper flights **and** a free China stopover.

## How the savings work

Chinese carriers compete aggressively on transpacific routes. Here's what you typically see:

| Route | Nonstop price | Via China | Savings |
|---|---|---|---|
| New York → Tokyo | $900-1,200 | $650-900 (via Shanghai) | $200-300 |
| Los Angeles → Seoul | $800-1,100 | $550-800 (via Beijing) | $200-300 |
| London → Bangkok | $600-900 | $400-650 (via Guangzhou) | $150-250 |
| Frankfurt → Tokyo | $700-1,000 | $500-750 (via Shanghai) | $150-250 |

*Prices are illustrative ranges based on typical economy fares. Actual prices vary by season and booking window.*

The savings come from Chinese carriers using their hubs as connecting points. Instead of paying for a direct flight, you route through Shanghai, Beijing, or Guangzhou — and get a stopover city thrown in.

## How to book it

### Option 1: Multi-city booking (recommended)

Book a **multi-city itinerary** instead of a round trip:

1. Leg 1: Your home city → Shanghai (or Beijing/Guangzhou)
2. Leg 2: Shanghai → Your final destination (Tokyo, Seoul, Bangkok, etc.)

Set the dates 3-5 days apart. You'll get the same cheap China-routing fare, but with a built-in stopover.

Most booking engines (Google Flights, Skyscanner, Kayak) support multi-city searches. Look for China Eastern (Shanghai hub), Air China (Beijing hub), or China Southern (Guangzhou hub).

### Option 2: Extended layover

Some airlines let you extend a layover at no additional cost. When booking, look for flights with a 12-24 hour layover in a Chinese city, then call the airline to ask about extending it to several days. This varies by airline and fare class.

### Option 3: Book two separate one-way tickets

If multi-city pricing is weird, book:
- One-way: Home → Chinese city
- One-way: Chinese city → Final destination (3-5 days later)

This sometimes comes out cheaper and gives you maximum flexibility.

## The visa-free part

Here's why this works so well: you don't need a Chinese visa for this stopover. The **240-hour transit visa-free policy** lets citizens of 55 countries (including all of the US, EU, UK, Japan, Korea, Australia, etc.) stay up to 10 days.

Requirements:
- Your entry country and exit country must be **different** (e.g., US → China → Japan works; Japan → China → Japan does not)
- You need a **confirmed onward ticket**
- Your passport must be valid for 6+ months

That's it. No application, no fee, no consulate visit. [Check your eligibility here](/check).

## Best stopover combinations

### For Japan travelers: Shanghai stopover (3 days)

- **Route:** US → Shanghai (3 days) → Tokyo
- **Why Shanghai:** Direct flights from all major US cities, most foreigner-friendly Chinese city, incredible food scene, The Bund waterfront is stunning at night
- **Cost:** Shanghai hotels from $30-60/night; meals from $3-10; metro rides $0.50

### For Korea travelers: Beijing stopover (3 days)

- **Route:** US → Beijing (3 days) → Seoul
- **Why Beijing:** Great Wall, Forbidden City, hutong neighborhoods, Peking duck. The historical highlights of China packed into a few days
- **Cost:** Beijing hotels from $25-50/night; meals from $2-8; Great Wall tour $30-50

### For Southeast Asia travelers: Guangzhou stopover (2 days)

- **Route:** US/EU → Guangzhou (2 days) → Bangkok/Bali/Vietnam
- **Why Guangzhou:** Dim sum capital of the world, vibrant night markets, day trip to Shenzhen's tech scene. China Southern hub with tons of Southeast Asia connections
- **Cost:** Guangzhou hotels from $20-40/night; dim sum brunch $5-10

### For adventurous travelers: Chengdu stopover (4 days)

- **Route:** US → Chengdu (4 days) → Tokyo/Seoul/Bangkok
- **Why Chengdu:** Giant panda base, legendary Sichuan hotpot, tea culture, relaxed vibe. Chengdu's airport grants **nationwide access** so you could train to Xi'an for the Terracotta Warriors
- **Cost:** Chengdu hotels from $20-40/night; meals from $2-6

## Before you go: 3 things to prepare

China's daily infrastructure works differently from most Western countries. A little prep goes a long way:

1. **Get connected.** Google, WhatsApp, and most Western apps are blocked in China. [Get an eSIM](/esim) before you land so you have data on arrival.

2. **Set up payments.** China is nearly cashless. Most vendors take WeChat Pay and Alipay only. [Set up mobile payments](/pay) before your trip.

3. **Download essential apps.** WeChat (messaging + payments), Alipay (payments), DiDi (ride-hailing), and a maps app that works in China. [Get the full app list](/apps).
